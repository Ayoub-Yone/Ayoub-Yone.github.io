YEAGERATTACK ON TITAN

Eren Yeager is the best-written character in anime.

Read why

Eren begins with a single wish: to be free. What makes him remarkable is how far the story follows that idea. The boy who screams about destroying his enemies gradually learns that the world is far more complicated than he thought, and the story never lets him, or the viewer, off easily.

What defines him is determination. However many times he is beaten, betrayed or broken, he keeps moving forward: “If I don’t keep moving forward, nothing will change.”

Freedom is what separates him from other protagonists. He doesn’t want strength or power for their own sake, and that one idea drives every choice he makes, including the ones that are hard to forgive.

The writing rewards a second viewing. The angry kid of the first season and the cold, distant Eren of the later ones are the same person, and many early scenes change meaning once you know where he ends up.

He is also far more calculating than he first appears: he adapts, learns from his enemies and acts on the information he has. Yet Mikasa and Armin never stop mattering to him, and that is what makes him tragic. He is not simply good or evil, only a person making increasingly extreme choices in a brutal world.

Eren can see the future he is heading toward, and he walks into it anyway, knowing what it will cost. You can reject his actions and still understand how he got there, which is why the debate around him never ends.

Eren isn’t the greatest because he is the strongest.

He is the greatest because his story forces you to ask what freedom, morality and sacrifice really mean.

From “I will kill all the Titans” to one of the biggest philosophical debates in anime.

That’s why he’s my GOAT.
